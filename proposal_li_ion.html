1. Executive Summary

The massive transition of India’s transport sector toward electric vehicles (EVs) is triggering an exponential surge in battery waste. The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) has enforced the **Battery Waste Management Rules 2022**, legally mandating that battery manufacturers and automobile OEMs recover and recycle active raw commodities. This shift marks the end of unorganized, informal lead-acid smashing and introduces a highly structured, digital-first recycling landscape.

The proposed **Ahmedabad Lithium-Ion Battery & Electronic Waste Recycling Facility (ALRF)** is a specialized processing plant designed to tap into this high-margin market. By establishing the facility with an initial CapEx of ₹35 Lakhs, ALRF deploys physical dismantling, mechanical shredding, and separator sieving systems to convert raw spent batteries and electronic circuit cards into high-purity **Black Mass** (containing Cobalt, Lithium, Nickel, and Manganese).

ALRF operates on a dual-monetization framework. First, the plant sells physical black mass directly to major hydrometallurgical chemical refineries at LME-indexed pricing spreads. Second, the facility registers its processing capacity on the CPCB Portal, generating digital **EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility) Certificates** which are traded to battery producers who must legally purchase these credits to offset their compliance targets.

Core Business Intent

By establishing a compliant Red-Category industrial recycling footprint, ALRF secures long-term institutional supply agreements with telecom, logistics, and automobile firms, securing a premium position in the secondary raw material sector in Western India.

2. Future Potential of the Industry

The battery recycling market is growing rapidly. Industry projections estimate that by 2030, spent battery volume in India will cross 100 GWh, driven by electric two-wheelers, telecom backup packs, and utility-scale energy storage systems. The growth curve is exponential, fueled by state-level mandates and corporate carbon-neutral targets.

Furthermore, electronic waste (E-Waste) is expanding at a CAGR of 15% in Gujarat alone, driven by rapid technology lifecycles and corporate data center upgrades in GIFT City and Ahmedabad. Circuit boards contain high concentrations of Gold, Silver, Copper, and Palladium, yielding high recovery spreads per tonne of processed waste.

Legal Recovery Targets under Battery Waste Management Rules 2022

Recyclers registered under the national portal must meet strict material recovery targets calculated based on the dry weight of raw batteries. Under the active schedule:
* **EV & Portable Batteries:** Recovery targets scale from **70% in 2024–25** up to **90% in 2026–27** (the current period).
* **Automotive & Industrial Lead-Acid Batteries:** Mandated at **95% recovery** target.
* **Recycled Content Obligations:** Battery manufacturers are legally required to integrate a minimum of **5% domestically recycled cobalt/lithium in 2027–28**, rising to **20% by 2030–31**. This guarantees long-term purchase demand for ALRF materials.

The announcement of **ASML partnering with Tata Electronics** to establish India's first commercial semiconductor fabrication facility in the **Dholera Special Investment Region (DSIR)** marks a structural shift in regional technology supply chains. This development directly impacts electronic and chemical recycling:

  • Lithium UPS Backups: The heavy industrial clean rooms and testing laboratories in Dholera require large-scale Lithium-Ion energy storage systems (ESS) for power backup. These battery banks will require cyclical recycling replacements starting in 2030.

The planned infrastructure projects—including massive EV shuttle fleets, smart transport nodes, and temporary high-density digital broadcast structures—will generate thousands of tonnes of temporary battery and communications electronic waste during clean-up and decommissioning phases, offering immediate local collection contracts.

12. Hydrometallurgical Extraction

As ALRF scales, we will move from dry mechanical shredding to chemical extraction. This process deploys acid dissolution and solvent extraction steps to refine black mass into 99.8% pure Cobalt Sulfate and Lithium Carbonate crystals, bypassing intermediate refineries to double net margins.

The hydrometallurgical leaching process follows the chemical reaction:

2 LiCoO2 + 3 H2SO4 + H2O2 → Li2SO4 + 2 CoSO4 + 4 H2O + O2 ↑

By reacting the black mass with **dilute sulfuric acid (H2SO4)** in the presence of **hydrogen peroxide (H2O2)** as a reducing agent, we achieve extraction yields exceeding 98.5% for both Lithium and Cobalt. The solution is subsequently treated with organic solvents (such as D2EHPA) to separate Nickel and Cobalt salts prior to final crystallization.

D2EHPA solvent extraction operates on a pH-dependent separation factor:
* **pH 2.5 - 3.0:** Cobalt is selectively extracted into the organic phase, leaving Nickel in the aqueous raffinate.
* **Stripping:** The Cobalt-loaded organic phase is stripped with dilute sulfuric acid to yield concentrated Cobalt Sulfate crystals.

13. Environmental, Health & Safety (EHS) Protocols

Worker health is monitored under strict DISH standards. Segregation workers are provided with chemical aprons, oxygen-assisted face masks, and undergo quarterly blood level checks for Lead and Cobalt exposure.

Our on-site EHS targets are structured to prevent chemical contact hazards:

  • Fluoride Emission Control: The thermal decomposition of LiPF6 electrolytes releases toxic Hydrogen Fluoride (HF) gas. Slicing and shredding machines are enclosed under negative pressure ducts that route vapors through a wet caustic soda (NaOH) scrubber system.
  • Scrubber Dosing Parameters: The caustic soda scrubber must maintain a constant pH of **10.5 to 11.5** using an automated NaOH dosing pump, preventing HF emissions from breaching the GPCB limit of **5 mg/Nm3**.
  • Workplace Air Quality Audits: Air quality inside the shed must be monitored for heavy metal dust. Continuous PM2.5 and PM10 monitors are linked to high-volume HEPA filters.
  • First-Aid Neutralization: Stock calcium gluconate gel at all workstations to neutralize any HF acid skin contact immediately.
