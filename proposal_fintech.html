1. Executive Summary

GIFT City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City) has emerged as India’s gate to global financial markets. Supported by a 10-year 100% corporate tax holiday, exemption from GST, and the absence of STT/CTT on offshore transactions, GIFT City provides a highly competitive regulatory environment for quantitative trading desks.

The proposed **GIFT City FinTech Algo-Trading & Arbitrage Desk (GFTD)** is a proprietary trading firm designed to capture pricing inefficiencies across national and international markets. Operating with a Phase 1 capital of ₹25 Lakhs, the desk utilizes low-latency co-location servers at the GIFT Data Center to execute statistical arbitrage, index spread trades, and option volatility strategies.

To manage risk during the initial setup phase, the company will operate under the **IFSCA FinTech Sandbox Framework**. This allows GFTD to test its algorithmic models in a live market environment with reduced capital requirements, transitioning to a full commercial license as trading volumes scale.

Core Business Intent

By establishing a low-latency trading desk in the GIFT City Special Economic Zone (SEZ), GFTD secures a tax-advantaged platform to run high-frequency arbitrage models on dollar-denominated contracts, capturing steady returns.

2. Future Potential of the Industry

The trading volume on GIFT City exchanges (such as NSE IX and India INX) is expanding rapidly, with daily volumes regularly exceeding $20B. The migration of the SGX Nifty contract to the **GIFT Nifty** has consolidated liquidity, attracting international institutional players.

As liquidity increases, pricing anomalies between domestic indices and GIFT City contracts create robust arbitrage opportunities. Quantitative firms with low-latency infrastructure are well-positioned to capture these spreads.

12. Quantitative Arbitrage Mechanics

Our algorithms trade the price spread between GIFT Nifty Futures and domestic NSE Nifty Futures. We model this relationship using co-integration analysis.

If $Y_t$ represents the price of GIFT Nifty Futures and $X_t$ represents the domestic Nifty Futures, we test the co-integrating relationship:

Y_t = β * X_t + ε_t

We use the Engle-Granger two-step method to confirm co-integration. First, we estimate the parameters using ordinary least squares (OLS). Second, we run an Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) unit root test on the residuals $\epsilon_t$ to confirm stationarity:

Δε_t = γ * ε_{t-1} + ∑ (φ_i * Δε_{t-i}) + u_t

If the residual series is stationary ($\gamma < 0$ and statistically significant), we construct a mean-reverting spread portfolio. When the spread deviates from its historical mean by more than two standard deviations, the system automatically enters long-short positions, closing the trade when the spread converges back to its mean.
