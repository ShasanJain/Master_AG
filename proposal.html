1. Executive Summary

The recycling and industrial scrap management sector in India has crossed a critical inflection point. Historically dominated by fragmented, unorganized players operating out of unlicensed yards, the industry is rapidly transitioning toward formalization. This shift is driven directly by new environmental laws, strict GST controls, and secondary steel manufacturing requirements that demand certified, high-grade scrap inputs.

The proposed **Ahmedabad Sustainable Scrap & Recycling Facility (ASRF)** represents a strategic opportunity to capture market share within one of India's most highly dense industrial casting and foundry states. The facility is modeled on a rigorous **"Capital Reservation Philosophy"**. Instead of investing heavy capital upfront on massive machinery and industrial land, ASRF initiates operations in Phase 1 as an **Asset-Light Aggregator & Sorting Center** with a small budget (₹5.2 Lakhs).

This phased launch validates localized supply lines, secures municipal and environmental clearances, and builds a stable customer portfolio using low overheads. Once cash flow matches operational targets, ASRF moves into Phase 2 by utilizing government-subsidized debt (PMEGP & CGTMSE) to purchase automated vertical/horizontal baling and shredding equipment, scaling the monthly throughput from 6 Tonnes to 40+ Tonnes.

Core Business Intent

By supplying clean, graded, and highly densified ferrous metals (steel, iron) and high-value non-ferrous metals (copper, aluminum, brass) directly to secondary steel producers and casting foundries in Vatva, Naroda, and Odhav GIDCs, ASRF establishes a reliable, recurring B2B trading framework.

2. Future Potential of the Industry

India's national recycling industry is riding structural tailwinds that guarantee long-term volume growth. The domestic metal recycling market, valued at **USD 11.4 Billion in 2024**, is projected to grow at a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of **6.8% to 7.3%**, reaching **USD 21 Billion by 2030**.

Key Structural Growth Drivers

  • National Steel Policy (NSP): The government’s mandate aims to increase domestic steel manufacturing capacity to 300 million tonnes by 2030. To meet these targets without causing excessive carbon footprints, secondary steel manufacturers must utilize recycled steel scrap in Electric Arc Furnaces (EAF) and Induction Furnaces (IF).
  • The "Green Steel" Carbon Directive: Global environmental standards, notably the European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), penalize high-carbon steel exports. Indian steel exporters are rapidly converting their smelting systems to run on scrap steel inputs, which reduce raw emissions by 58% compared to virgin ore processing.
  • The Vehicle Scrappage Policy: The phased ban on old, unfit private and commercial vehicles (older than 15–20 years) creates a continuous domestic supply of low-cost, high-grade automotive steel and copper scrap, driving prices down for recyclers.
  • The EPR Credit Market Explosion: The Central Pollution Control Board’s digital registry forces major manufacturing brands to purchase Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) credits from authorized recyclers, generating a digital, high-margin secondary income.

4. National & Local Competitors

Entering this space requires understanding the operational structures of both national corporate leaders and organized local recycling groups.

National Corporate Pioneers

  • Tata Steel Recycling (Tata FerroBaled / FerroShred):
    *Model:* Operates large, highly automated, multi-acre shredding hubs (such as their flagship facility in Rohtak, Haryana). Sourced scrap is crushed, processed, sorted magnetically, and compressed into uniform, high-density blocks branded as *FerroBaled*. Sourcing is managed digitally via their *FerroHaat* B2B app.
    *Takeaway for ASRF:* We cannot compete on high-tonnage shredding. However, we can act as a feeder system, using manual sorting to supply high-purity scrap to secondary smelting hubs that corporate players cannot service flexibly.
  • Mahindra Accelo (CERO Recycling):
    *Model:* A joint venture with government-owned MSTC. Cero is India's leading Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility (RVSF). They focus on extracting metals, rubber, glass, and copper wiring from end-of-life cars and commercial vehicles.
    *Takeaway for ASRF:* Cero focuses strictly on automotive scrapping. We target localized industrial fabrication scrap, steel shavings, and packaging materials that offer higher turnover speeds and lighter regulatory pipelines.
  • CMR Green Technologies & Gravita India:
    *Model:* CMR is the undisputed national leader in aluminum casting alloy recycling, while Gravita specializes in lead and aluminum battery smelting.
    *Takeaway for ASRF:* Illustrates the massive profit potential of specialized non-ferrous metals. ASRF will prioritize separating and grading copper and aluminum, as these represent only 15% of our raw volume but will contribute to 45% of our net margins.

Local Gujarat Competitors

  • Kutch Green Recycling (Gandhidham / Anjar): Large, port-adjacent sorting and processing yards. Focuses primarily on heavy maritime and structural steel scrap from ship-breaking yards.

    9.1 Risk-Shielded Asset Strategies & Joint Ventures

    Heavy scrap processing machinery (hydraulic balers, alligator shears, crane loaders) represents a double-edged sword for a startup. They require significant upfront capital expenditure (₹12 Lakhs – ₹25 Lakhs) and suffer from rapid depreciation (typically 15% to 30% per annum under the Indian Income Tax Act). If raw sourcing volumes fluctuate, fixed machinery debt and depreciation will quickly drain working capital.

    To eliminate this asset risk while retaining 100% of ASRF’s growth potential, we will deploy four defensive capital protection strategies:

    1

    Toll Processing & Job-Work Partnerships

    Outsourcing Capital-Heavy Compacting

    Instead of purchasing a baler, ASRF will partner with under-utilized plastic recycling and metal fabrication units in Odhav and Kathwada. These yards have heavy-duty hydraulic baling presses running at under 40% capacity. We aggregate and sort raw metals at our open yard, then route them to their facilities under a **"Job-Work Toll Contract"** (paying a flat ₹800 – ₹1,200 per tonne processed).

    *Benefit:* Completely bypasses the ₹12L baler CapEx, transforming fixed machinery debt into a variable per-tonne fee while ASRF retains 100% of sales branding and margins.

    2

    Distressed Asset Acquisitions

    Buying Closed-Yard Liquidations

    Many local scrap yards and fabrication workshops face closure due to cash flow insolvencies or tax non-compliance. ASRF will actively source second-hand machinery (platform scales, crocodile shears, wire strippers) from liquidation brokers and MSTC India public auction portals.

    *Benefit:* Secure fully functional, certified industrial recycling machinery at **40% to 60% below book value**, avoiding dealer markups and drastically accelerating capital payback.

    3

    Foundry Co-Location Joint Venture

    Shared Machinery Integration

    Partner with an existing, mid-scale melting foundry in Chhatral or Vatva GIDC that possesses under-utilized industrial weighbridges, overhead gantry cranes, and massive shearing machinery. ASRF co-locates our sorting operations inside their vacant boundary acreage.

    *Benefit:* In exchange for a guaranteed first-right-of-refusal to purchase our sorted scrap at a fixed 3% discount (retaining their transport costs), they allow ASRF to use their cranes and heavy shears for free during their night shifts, reducing our Phase 2 CapEx to zero.

    4

    Operational B2B Leasing Models

    Shifting CapEx to Tax-Deductible OpEx

    For necessary material handlers and high-capacity balers, ASRF will reject outright purchasing. Instead, we will contract with B2B machinery lessors (such as Tata Capital B2B leasing or regional machinery distributors) under a 3-year operating lease.

    *Benefit:* Preserves our cash reserve. Monthly lease payments are treated as 100% tax-deductible operational expenditure (OpEx), lowering net corporate tax liability without weighing down our balance sheet with depreciating assets.

    10. EPR Credit Monetization

    Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) represents a massive paradigm shift in Indian recycling monetization. The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) mandates that any brand using plastic packaging or manufacturing electronic goods (PIBOs - Producers, Importers, Brand Owners) must legally purchase EPR credits to offset their environmental footprint.

    EPR Digital Credit Generation Pathway

    1. CPCB Registration: ASRF registers as an authorized waste processor on the CPCB Portal, uploading our GPCB Consent to Operate (CTO) and weighing capacity metrics.
    2. Verification of Inflow: As we collect and aggregate post-consumer hard plastics (PET, HDPE bottles) and electronic waste components, all purchases and sales are recorded via GST-compliant e-way bills.
    3. Digital Credit Issuance: CPCB algorithmically issues 1 digital **EPR Credit** on the portal for every 1 Tonne of verified recycling processed.
    4. Credit Trading Board: ASRF lists these credits on the CPCB trading board, where major corporate entities (such as Reliance, PepsiCo, ITC) bid and purchase them directly, yielding a pure profit margin of **₹2,000 to ₹5,000 per Tonne** without physical product delivery.

    12. Global Sourcing & Scrap Import Mechanics

    As ASRF scales into Phase 3, relying solely on local workshop scrap limits daily throughput. To reach industrial-scale viability, the facility will establish a structured international supply line to import high-purity metallic waste from the United States, the United Kingdom, and the European Union.

    Gujarat offers a strategic geopolitical advantage for global scrap trade. The state features two of India’s largest deep-water ports—**Mundra Port** (Adani Special Economic Zone) and **Kandla Port** (Deendayal Port Authority)—both of which are formally designated by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) as licensed clearing ports for metallic waste imports.

    DGFT Compliance & Exemption Pathways

    Importing scrap into India is highly regulated to prevent the dumping of hazardous, explosive, or radioactive materials. ASRF will execute imports under the following statutory frameworks:

    • Pre-Shipment Inspection Certificate (PSIC) Exemption: Under DGFT Public Notice guidelines, metallic scrap consignments imported from "Safe Regions" (specifically the US, UK, Canada, EU, Australia, and New Zealand) are exempted from the requirement of a Pre-Shipment Inspection Agency (PSIA) certificate at the port of origin.
    • Supplier Radiation Declarations: To utilize the PSIC exemption, each imported container must be accompanied by a formal declaration from the scrap yard authority at the origin confirming that the consignment contains zero radioactive or explosive residues.
    • Mandatory Port Scans: Upon arrival at Mundra or Kandla, containers undergo automated portal radiation monitor scans and physical container x-ray checks conducted by Indian Customs before final domestic clearance.
    • Customs House Agent (CHA) Integration: Establish a dedicated account with a licensed CHA at Gandhidham to manage the filing of the Bill of Entry on the ICEGATE portal, ensuring immediate clearance to avoid container demurrage charges (which can run up to ₹15,000 per container per day).

    13. Secondary Smelting & Ingot Export Framework

    Transitioning from a pure raw metal aggregator to a value-added manufacturer represents the ultimate margin-expansion strategy for ASRF. In Phase 3, we will install localized induction and rotary smelting furnaces to process raw sorted aluminum and copper scrap into standardized metallurgical ingots.

    Value-Added Products

    Instead of selling loose aluminum castings at low scrap value (₹140/kg), ASRF will smelt and alloy them into standard ADC12 Aluminum Die-Casting Alloys or LM6 Aluminum Ingots (valued at ₹210+/kg). These alloys are purchased in bulk by automotive OEM parts manufacturers in Sanand GIDC and international buyers in South-East Asia.

    Export Incentives

    By refining non-ferrous metals to 99.7% purity, ASRF gains access to high-margin export markets in Europe and China. Under the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), European buyers pay a substantial pricing premium for certified secondary (recycled) metal ingots because they carry a fraction of the carbon footprint of virgin-smelted metals.

    DGFT Advance Authorization & SION Guidelines

    ASRF will utilize the **Advance Authorization Scheme** to import raw metallic scrap duty-free. Under this scheme, the government waives the basic customs duty on imported inputs, provided the material is processed and exported as finished metal ingots within 18 months. Operations must strictly adhere to the pre-fixed **Standard Input-Output Norms (SION)**, which mandate precise conversion yield ratios (e.g., maximum permissible process melting loss of 4% for copper and 7% for aluminum scrap).

    14. Advanced Yard Sorting Technologies

    As operational volumes cross 100 Tonnes per month, manual sorting by hand segregators becomes an efficiency bottleneck. Phase 3 of ASRF integrates automated sensor-based sorting systems to secure near-100% material purity:

    1

    X-Ray Transmission (XRT)

    Deploys high-speed x-ray sensors to analyze the atomic density of incoming scrap on a conveyor belt. Instantly segregates heavy metals (copper, brass, zinc) from light aluminum alloys, sorting up to 5 Tonnes per hour with zero human labor.

    2

    Near-Infrared (NIR) Sorting

    Essential for polymer sorting. Measures the light absorption spectrum of moving plastic fragments to instantly differentiate and sort HDPE, PET, PP, and PVC polymers, securing 99.8% pure polymer streams for GPCB/CPCB compliance.

    3

    LIBS Technology

    Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy uses nanosecond laser pulses to vaporize micro-points of alloy scrap. Instantly reads precise chemical percentages (e.g., separating 6000-series from 5000-series aluminum), allowing premium alloy pricing.

    15. Environmental, Health & Safety (EHS) Protocols

    Operating a heavy industrial recycling yard carrying toxic or flammable components presents serious compliance liabilities if unmanaged. ASRF enforces a rigorous, zero-compromise EHS framework:

    GPCB Water & Ground Pollution Barriers

    To prevent toxic heavy metals and battery acids from leaching into the local Ahmedabad water table, the yard layout integrates the following physical structures:

    • Impermeable Concrete Containment Beds: All battery storage, sorting, and cutting areas must feature a 6-inch thick, epoxy-coated concrete floor. This prevents any chemical spill or acidic battery fluid from making direct ground contact.
    • Spill Catchment Trenches: The perimeter of the non-ferrous processing floor is bordered by chemical-resistant collection trenches leading to a central holding tank for neutralization processing.
    • Rainwater Run-off Isolation: The yard features physical diversion bunds that separate clean storm rainwater from the scrap storage zone, preventing stormwater contamination in compliance with GPCB Consent rules.

    Worker Safety & Yard Operations

    All operations under the DISH Factories Act regulations must enforce strict safety rules: steel-toed boots, heavy leather sorting gloves, eye-protection goggles for metal cutters, and designated, color-coded safety walkways that isolate workers from moving forklift trucks and baling areas.
