# Brief: Ancient Indian Sculpture (4,000 Years of Form and Spirit)

## Premise
A procedural, 45-second visual essay in three-ink Risograph print (Teal, Fluorescent Pink, Yellow over cream paper). Traces the evolutionary leap of Indian sculptural genius across 4,000 years: from the defiant lost-wax bronze gesture of the Indus Valley, through Mauryan mirror-polished monumental stone, Gupta meditative transcendence, and Elephanta colossal rock relief, culminating in the cosmic motion of Chola bronze.

## Visual Device
**The Transforming Circle**:
- Starts as the heavy bronze arm bangles of the Indus Dancing Girl.
- Morphs into the 24-spoke Ashoka Chakra wheel of Sarnath.
- Expands into the radiant lotus halo (*Prabhamandala*) of the Preaching Buddha.
- Solidifies into the dark monolithic basalt halo of the Elephanta Trimurti.
- Ignites into the roaring ring of cosmic fire (*Tiruvasi*) framing Shiva Nataraja.

## Historical Facts & Verified References
1. **Mohenjo-daro Dancing Girl (c. 2500–2300 BCE)**:
   - Excavated at Mohenjo-daro (Sindh) in 1926 by Ernest Mackay.
   - Cast via lost-wax (*cire perdue*) bronze. Stands 10.5 cm in a confident, rhythmically poised *tribhanga* stance with 24 bangles on her left arm.
   - Reference: National Museum, New Delhi; Mackay, E. *Further Excavations at Mohenjo-daro* (1938).
2. **Ashokan Lion Capital at Sarnath (c. 250 BCE)**:
   - Commissioned by Emperor Ashoka at the site of Buddha's First Sermon (Deer Park, Sarnath).
   - Carved from a single monolith of Chunar sandstone with a lustrous, glassy mirror polish. Features four addorsed Asiatic lions resting on an abacus with the 24-spoked Dharma Chakra.
   - Reference: Archaeological Museum Sarnath; Coomaraswamy, A. K. *History of Indian and Indonesian Art* (1927).
3. **Sarnath Preaching Buddha (c. 5th Century CE)**:
   - Masterpiece of classical Gupta sculpture depicting the *Dharmachakra Pravartana Mudra* (Turning the Wheel of the Law).
   - Features the clinging transparent drapery style, carved celestial halo, and meditative introspection.
   - Reference: Sarnath Museum; Harle, J. C. *The Art and Architecture of the Indian Subcontinent* (1994).
4. **Elephanta Trimurti / Sadashiva (c. 6th–8th Century CE)**:
   - Colossal 6-meter rock-cut relief carved into the basalt cave at Elephanta Island, Mumbai harbor.
   - Depicts the three aspects of the Supreme Divinity: central tranquil *Tatpurusha*, fierce masculine *Aghora* (holding a cobra), and gentle feminine *Vamadeva* (holding a lotus).
   - Reference: UNESCO World Heritage Site No. 244; Kramrisch, Stella. *The Presence of Siva* (1981).
5. **Chola Shiva Nataraja (c. 10th–12th Century CE)**:
   - Imperial Chola lost-wax bronze (*panchaloha*).
   - Depicts the *Ananda Tandava* (Cosmic Dance of Bliss) within a flaming circle (*prabhamandala*). Four arms balance creation (*damaru* drum) and destruction (*agni* flame), offering protection (*abhaya*) and liberation (*gaja-hasta*), while subduing ignorance (*Apasmara*).
   - Reference: Government Museum, Chennai; Sivaramamurti, C. *Nataraja in Art, Thought and Literature* (1974).
